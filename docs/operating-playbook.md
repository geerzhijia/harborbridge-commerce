# Workflow
1. Owner supplies target market, risk appetite, budget, entity/jurisdiction and product exclusions. CEO drafts bounded tasks. Owner approves each task in the local ledger.
2. Market specialist proposes three niches with dated evidence from accessible sources, time windows and uncertainty. CEO submits a shortlist decision packet to owner.
3. Compliance reviews each candidate before sourcing commitments. Flag regulatory complexity, restricted categories, IP and missing tests; obtain specialist advice when needed.
4. Sourcing compares factories and samples on identical specifications. Finance models low/base/high cases and launch cash. All supplier communications, sample purchases and orders need separate owner approval.
5. CEO consolidates demand, supplier risk, economics and compliance into a go/no-go packet. Owner approves the specific product, supplier, quantity and budget or requests changes.
6. Logistics prepares the shipment; Amazon prepares listing and FBA plan. Owner separately approves booking, payment, customs instructions and publication. Reconcile actual costs against estimates.
7. After launch, approved monitoring tasks review account health, inventory, reviews and shipment exceptions. Reports must state last observation time; a saved prompt alone does not monitor anything.

Each task should have a single owner, acceptance criteria, dependencies, approved scope and a checkpoint. Save reports at reports/<task-id>.md. Keep source records with URL, access time, geography, period, currency, quote validity, confidence and archived evidence when permitted. CEO proposes responsibility changes with a diff and rationale; owner approval precedes implementation.

Finance must account for minimum order inventory and cash timing, not merely per-unit margins. Track recoverable versus nonrecoverable taxes, duty basis and tariffs separately with broker input; do not infer classification from a product nickname.
